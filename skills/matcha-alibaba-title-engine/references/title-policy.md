# Title Policy

## Product identity gate

Identify the SKU before writing. Additional views of the same color, bowl, case, and accessory arrangement supplement the current SKU; they do not justify a second title or ledger row.

Record the full visible configuration, but do not force every accessory into the title. Common components include a matcha bowl or chawan, bamboo whisk or chasen, whisk holder, chashaku, sifter, tea cloth, tray, cup, spoon, gift box, and carrying case. Prioritize search-bearing product nouns and distinctive purchase features over low-value accessory enumeration.

Handle variable 2–11-piece assortments from the actual SKU facts:

- Include a piece count only when the photographed/listed SKU is fixed and the count is verified.
- Omit the count for customizable assortments, mixed variants, or unclear images.
- Never infer count from layout alone; packaging inserts, lids, cups, cloths, and holders may be counted differently by suppliers.

Resolve image/text conflicts conservatively. For example, use `Snowflake Glaze` for verified 落雪釉 wording, but do not translate 翡翠 as `Jade` or `Emerald` when the actual SKU is pink or the term describes a glaze series rather than color. Do not claim Japanese origin; use `Japanese Style` only when it is accurate and useful.

## Hard title rules

- Maximum length: 128 characters including spaces and punctuation. Target 105–125 when natural; 126–128 is acceptable when every word earns its place.
- Use Title Case. Keep these project function words lowercase unless first: `the`, `a`, `an`, `and`, `but`, `for`, `nor`, `or`, `so`, `yet`, `if`, `at`, `by`, `in`, `of`, `on`, `to`, `up`, `with`. Keep common acronyms such as OEM and ODM uppercase.
- Begin with a truthful procurement or customization expression such as `Custom Logo`, `Customized`, `Personalized`, `OEM`, `ODM`, or `Factory Supplier`.
- Do not lead with or insert `US Stock`, `USA Stock`, or `In Stock` under this project’s current rule. Emphasize customization instead.
- Avoid special promotional punctuation, emojis, `@`, `!`, and `?`.
- Do not repeat synonyms merely to add keywords. Repeating `Matcha` is acceptable only when needed to preserve distinct exact phrases such as `Matcha Travel Set`, `Matcha Bowl`, and `Matcha Whisk`.
- Use only verified material, capacity, dimensions, finish, color, structure, components, and use cases.
- Never promise food safety, certification, origin, handmade status, or included accessories without evidence.

## Keyword and title architecture

Before selecting the primary phrase, complete the fresh image-specific checks in [live-market-research.md](live-market-research.md). Build queries from the verified SKU facts—exact product noun, material or finish, distinctive structure, configuration, and packaging—rather than reusing a generic category search or research completed for a different product.

Use this priority order:

1. One protected exact core phrase with the strongest qualified traffic opportunity for the product, such as `Matcha Set`, `Matcha Kit`, `Matcha Bowl`, `Matcha Whisk Set`, or `Matcha Travel Set`.
2. A second exact core or long-tail phrase only when natural, materially useful, and supported by evidence.
3. Two to four differentiators: glaze, spout, bowl form, leather case, tray, holder, gift packaging, or buyer-use attribute.
4. An uncovered or rising phrase only when it fits naturally in remaining space. It is optional and must not displace the protected primary phrase or a stronger conversion-bearing attribute.

Prefer this flexible structure:

`[Procurement Expression] [Primary Product Phrase] with [Distinctive Product Feature], [Portable/Packaging Feature], [Key Components]`

Do not mechanically fill every slot. Preserve natural buyer-readable English and the strongest exact phrase.

## Traffic-first selection

Product accuracy and relevance are hard gates. Among eligible phrases and candidate titles, use the provisional 100-point model in [traffic-learning.md](traffic-learning.md):

- Traffic potential: 45 points.
- Smoothed downstream conversion signal: 30 points.
- Evidence quality, comparability, and recency: 15 points.
- Differentiating fit: 10 points, of which uncovered-portfolio coverage may contribute no more than 5 points.

The A/B/C labels describe evidence/phrase roles; their old 3/2/1 coverage weights are not ranking scores. Use the newest comparable platform window for present selection and historical evidence for stability. Missing metrics remain unknown rather than zero. Never infer that one word caused the performance of a title containing several search phrases.

Lock the primary phrase before adding long tails. If duplicate control is triggered, first vary sentence architecture and verified differentiators. Do not replace a proven core phrase with a weak synonym merely to lower similarity or increase coverage.

## Output contract

Return:

1. A simple Chinese product name based on verified facts.
2. One final English title.
3. Exact character count.
4. A compact decision line naming the protected primary phrase, secondary entrance(s), user-data source date/window, live-research date/platforms, and key attributes. If a learned pattern or adjusted weight materially influenced the decision, also name its model version or evidence ID. Mention new coverage only when it actually influenced the title.
5. Maximum similarity to the latest five active formal titles, the contiguous-six-word result, and primary-phrase protection result.

Do not count a mere image upload as a generated title. Do not create a new output when a supplemental angle adds only secondary accessories and the current title remains optimal; state that the existing title is retained.
